01

At a glance

Textbooks give a pendulum's period as T = 2π√(L/g), and that formula never mentions how far the pendulum swings. This sim races a real pendulum against the formula, so you can see where the formula holds, where it fails, and by how much.

What you'll learn

  • Where T₀ = 2π√(L/g) comes from, and the approximation it rests on.
  • How the period of a real pendulum depends on its release angle θ₀, and why.
  • How to read the correction factor T/T₀ off the curve, and turn it into a period.
  • Why the length sets the time scale, while only the release angle sets the size of the formula's error.

04

Why the real pendulum runs late

  • On either side of the vertical, |sin θ| < |θ|, so the real pull back is weaker than the formula assumes.
  • Energy conservation makes this exact: at every angle the real bob moves more slowly than the ghost, so every real swing takes longer.
  • The wider the release, the bigger the gap, and the longer the swing.

06

What the length changes

In T = T₀ × (factor set by θ₀), the length appears only inside T₀ = 2π√(L/g).

  • Length sets the time scale. T₀ grows as √L, so going from 1 m to 3 m stretches every period by √3 ≈ 1.73.
  • The release angle sets the error. T/T₀ has no L in it, so the ghost's lead, counted in swings, is the same at any length.

07

Where it matters

Small-angle estimate of the extra time (θ₀ in radians).
  • Pendulum clocks. A clock is adjusted for its usual swing, but if the swing size drifts, its rate drifts too: from 2.0° to 2.2° is about 1.4 s a day. This is called circular error, and it is why clock swings are kept small and steady.
  • Huygens, who built the first pendulum clock, added curved guides (cycloidal cheeks) that shorten the string on wide swings. On a cycloid the period does not depend on the amplitude at all.
  • Measuring g. Timing a pendulum gives g, but a 10° swing already makes the answer about 0.4% too small unless you correct for it.

08

Summary

In one line. T₀ = 2π√(L/g) is the small-angle limit of the true period. Every real swing, released from rest, takes longer than it, by a fraction set only by the release angle.

Key points

  • T₀ = 2π√(L/g) comes from replacing sin θ by θ.
  • Since sin θ < θ, every real swing is longer than T₀, and more so at wider angles.
  • The real period is T₀ times a factor that depends only on θ₀ (read T/T₀ off the curve).
  • Length sets the time scale; the release angle alone sets the error.

Common mix-ups

  • "The period doesn't depend on amplitude." Only for very small swings.
  • "Wide swings are faster, because the bob moves faster." It does move faster. With a pull proportional to θ, the extra speed would exactly cover the longer path. The real pull, sin θ, grows more slowly, so the speed falls short and the swing takes longer.
  • "A longer pendulum makes the error worse." The fractional error does not depend on L.
