A ball of density ρ has its horizon radius equal to its own radius (Rs = r) at
r = √( 3c² / 8πGρ )
so 100× less dense means only 10× bigger. (It collapses a little before, at √(8/9) ≈ 94% of this radius.) Read backwards, a black hole's average density falls with its size: ρ = 3c² / (8πG Rs²). Bigger black holes are less dense: double the radius, one quarter the density.
